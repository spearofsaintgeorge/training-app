The mandate
13 weeks to the start line with Sam. Build muscle, build the engine, arrive durable. Leave reps in reserve when in doubt — a plan you run for 13 weeks beats a perfect one that breaks you in week 2. Pain shows up → back off and re-check.

The Architecture

13-Week Spartan Super Block

Anchored to the Spartan Super (Sept 26–27, ~10K / ~25 obstacles). Muscle-weighted — that's your goal — while building the grip, carries, and aerobic engine the course demands. After race day we pivot to a pure hypertrophy / longevity block.

The weekly engine

One structure absorbs everything: 4 lifting days (each muscle 2×/week), 3 conditioning touches, 2 yoga, the home pull-up tower greased through the week, and the sauna daily. This is the Phase 2 "Build" shape — Phase 1 trims a set and adds a rep in reserve; the taper halves it.

Lifting principles evidence-based

  • Volume drives growth. 10–20 hard sets per muscle per week, ramped across phases.
  • Frequency 2×. Every muscle trained twice weekly for better volume distribution.
  • Proximity to failure. Keep 1–3 reps in reserve (RIR); it matters more than the exact rep number.
  • Double progression. Hit the top of the rep range on all sets → add load. The logbook does this math for you.
  • Full range, controlled. ~2s lowering, full stretch — also builds flexibility under load.

Spartan layer woven in

  • Grip: dead hangs, pull-ups (your tower), farmer carries, plate pinches.
  • Carries / odd-object: farmer & suitcase carries, sandbag, sled if available.
  • Posterior chain + single-leg: RDLs, hip thrusts, split squats, step-ups — the hill engine.
  • Burpees: the penalty currency. We build tolerance, not dread.
  • Core, post-surgery-smart: anti-rotation/extension first → loaded carries & bracing. Heavy Valsalva is earned, not rushed.

The Engine

Conditioning — building the ~10K

A Super is roughly 10K of terrain with obstacles. We build an aerobic base (Zone 2), sharpen it with intervals (VO₂max — one of the strongest predictors of how long you live), and rehearse the race. Your hikes and walks are the base; the sauna heat-acclimates you for a warm California course.

Zone 2

Conversational pace — you can talk in full sentences, nose-breathing-ish. Builds the aerobic base that carries you between obstacles. This is most of your cardio.

VO₂max work

Hard 3–4 min efforts up hills with full recovery. Small dose, big return — the top-end engine and a major longevity lever. Once a week, never to failure early on.

Race rehearsal

In the peak weeks, mix carries, burpees, and crawls into your terrain runs so grip-while-gassed feels familiar on race day.

Nutrition

Protein, every meal

Spread your target across 3–5 feedings, ~0.4 g/kg each. A palm-or-two of protein per plate. This is the lever that turns training into muscle.

Carbs around work

Put most carbs near lifting and long conditioning days — they fuel the hard sessions and the race engine. Keep fat ≥0.3 g/lb for hormones.

Hydrate + salt

Daily sauna and conditioning pull water and sodium hard — and your gut history means fluid/electrolytes matter more, not less. Salt your food, add electrolytes on heavy days, sip around the sauna.

Keep the junk out of the house — the standing order holds. The plan isn't restriction; it's making the strong choice the easy one because the weak choice isn't in the pantry.

Durability

Mobility, Yoga & Warm-up

Full-range lifting already builds flexibility under load. These keep the desk-worker restrictions — hips, T-spine, shoulders, ankles — from becoming the thing that breaks down at 60.

Pre-lift warm-up ~8 min

  • 5 min easy cardio — bike / row / incline walk, break a light sweat.
  • Dynamic flow — 10 bodyweight squats, 10 leg swings/side, 10 arm circles, 10 band pull-aparts.
  • Ramp-up sets — 2–3 progressively heavier sets on your first big lift before the working weight.

Yoga days (2×/wk)

  • Wed — mobility flow: hips, hamstrings, T-spine rotation, shoulders. 20–30 min.
  • Sun — restorative: longer holds, breathing, downshift the nervous system. Pairs with the easy walk.
  • Core thread: dead bug, bird dog, side plank — the post-surgery-safe foundation that earns the heavy bracing later.

Daily threads

Walks

Morning steps, post-meal strolls. Free aerobic base and recovery — keep moving on rest days.

Pull-up tower

Grease the groove: a few sub-max sets through the day on non-pull days. Grip + lats compound quietly.

Sauna

15–20 min, most days. Recovery, cardiovascular adaptation, heat-prep for California. Hydrate around it.

Sleep

7–9 h. The single biggest recovery lever and it's already good — protect it like a board meeting.

No Black Boxes

The Why

You build platforms you understand. Same here — every choice in this system traces to evidence, not bro-lore.

Volume is the driver

Across the research, weekly hard-set volume (10–20 sets/muscle) is the strongest training lever for hypertrophy. We periodize it up across the block rather than maxing it day one — fatigue is the tax on volume.

Proximity to failure (RIR)

Growth happens across a wide rep range if sets are taken close to failure — within ~1–3 reps. RIR lets you autoregulate: harder when fresh, back off when the body says so. It's also the safety valve post-surgery.

Progressive overload

Muscle and strength adapt only to a rising demand. Double progression — add reps, then add load — is the simplest honest way to guarantee the line goes up. The logbook enforces it.

Protein & creatine

1.6–2.2 g/kg/day is the well-replicated range for maximizing muscle gain. Creatine monohydrate (5 g/day) is the most evidence-backed supplement there is — strength, size, and a cognitive bonus. Cheap, safe, proven.

Zone 2 + VO₂max

Cardiorespiratory fitness is among the strongest predictors of all-cause mortality — low fitness rivals smoking as a risk factor. A big Zone-2 base plus a weekly hard interval builds both the Spartan engine and the healthspan.

The sauna

Large Finnish cohorts link frequent sauna use to lower cardiovascular and all-cause mortality, with recovery and heat-adaptation benefits on top. Your daily habit is doing real work — we just hydrate around it.

Post-surgery ramp

Cleared for full activity, yes — but abdominal tissue earns heavy intra-abdominal pressure progressively. We start core with anti-rotation/anti-extension and build to braced heavy lifting. Pain shows up → back off and re-check.

Why anchor to the race

A 13-week deadline with Sam is a consistency machine. It keeps you lean (faster on course, healthier), forces the engine work most lifters skip, and you still grow. After the line, we let the muscle block run unconstrained.
