The Engine

Conditioning — the minimum effective dose

During the bulk, conditioning is maintenance: enough to keep the heart, the recovery engine and the longevity numbers moving — not so much that it eats the surplus or your legs. Two easy Zone-2 sessions a week. That's the job.

Zone 2

Conversational pace — full sentences, nose-breathing-ish. Incline walk, bike, rower or a hike. Put it on non-lifting days or after an upper day, never right before heavy legs.

Why so little?

Hard cardio competes with leg strength and burns the surplus you're eating to grow. Two easy sessions hold the aerobic base without the interference.

Walks are the background

Daily walks aren't conditioning sessions — they're the floor. Keep them; they help recovery, digestion and appetite, and cost nothing.

Carbs around work

Most of your carbs near the lifts — they fuel the heavy days and refill the tank after. Keep fat steady for hormones.

Hydrate + salt

The sauna and the lifting pull water and sodium. Salt your food, add electrolytes on hot or heavy days, and sip around the sauna.

Durability

Mobility, Yoga & Warm-up

Full-range lifting builds flexibility under load. These keep the hips, hamstrings, upper back and neck from becoming the thing that breaks down at 60.

Pre-lift warm-up ~8 min

  • 5 min easy cardio — bike, rower or incline walk; break a light sweat.
  • Dynamic flow — 10 bodyweight squats, 10 leg swings a side, 10 band pull-aparts, 10 chin tucks.
  • Ramp-up sets — gym mode builds the ramp for your main lift: empty bar → 40% → 60% → 75% → 85%.

Mobility 2× a week non-lifting days

  • Hips & hamstrings: 90-90 switches, hamstring floss, couch stretch. 15–20 min.
  • Upper back: T-spine rotations and extensions over a roller — the antidote to the desk.
  • Restorative: longer holds and slow breathing. A yoga class counts — add it on Today.

Back & core before lower days

  • The big three: curl-up, side plank, bird dog — 5 minutes, builds a stiff, quiet trunk.
  • Brace, don't arch: ribs down on every hinge and squat. Stop a set the rep your back takes over.
  • Hamstrings: strong, long hamstrings take load off the low back — that's why curls and RDLs are in every week.

Neck & traps daily, 3 min

  • Chin tucks 2×10 and a doorway pec stretch, 30 s a side.
  • Press seated and supported; no heavy shrugging. If the hack-squat pads bite, switch to the belt squat.
  • Face pulls every Upper B — the posture work that keeps the neck long.

No Black Boxes

The Why

You build platforms you understand. Same here — every choice traces to evidence, not bro-lore.

Strength and size, both

Heavy sets of 3–6 build strength; sets of 8–15 taken close to failure build size. Running both every week — two heavy days, two muscle days — is a well-supported way to get bigger and stronger at once, and each muscle still gets trained twice.

Volume is the driver

Weekly hard-set volume (10–20 sets per muscle) is the strongest training lever for growth. We ramp it across the block instead of maxing it day one — fatigue is the tax on volume.

Proximity to failure (RIR)

Growth happens across a wide rep range if sets end within ~1–3 reps of failure. Reps-in-reserve lets you autoregulate: push when fresh, back off when the body says so. The readiness check does this for you on flat days.

Progressive overload

Muscle and strength only adapt to a rising demand. Double progression — add reps, then add load — is the simplest honest way to make the line go up. On the pull-up, the load that drops is the assistance.

Planned deloads

Fatigue masks fitness. A light week every 4–6 weeks lets it clear so the next stretch starts higher. They land on Thanksgiving and Christmas weeks — the weeks life was going to deload you anyway.

Spine-friendly loading

The trap bar keeps the load in line with your hips, so the low back works less for the same weight. Hack and belt squats load the legs without a bar across the spine. Chest-supported rows take the back out of rowing. Same stimulus, less wear.

The pull-up ladder

Bigger lifters can't do many bodyweight reps, so we make the movement scalable: assistance that drops a notch at a time, timed hangs for grip and shoulders, and frequent easy practice on off days ("grease the groove"). Practice without fatigue builds the skill fastest.

Bulk without a scale

A modest surplus plus enough protein builds muscle; the scoreboard is the bar, not the scale. Four real meals with protein at each covers it without counting anything.

Protein & creatine

1.6–2.2 g/kg/day is the well-replicated range for maximizing muscle gain. Creatine monohydrate (5 g/day) is the most evidence-backed supplement there is — strength, size, and a cognitive bonus. Cheap, safe, proven.

Zone 2 + the sauna

Cardiorespiratory fitness is among the strongest predictors of all-cause mortality, and frequent sauna use is linked to lower cardiovascular risk in large cohorts. A small, steady dose of both keeps the healthspan work going while the lifting does the building.
